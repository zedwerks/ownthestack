OpenMRS began in February 2004, when Paul Biondich and Burke Mamlin of the Regenstrief Institute, working on a visit to the AMPATH HIV-care partnership in Eldoret, Kenya, started building a platform to replace paper charting for HIV patients. Partners In Health's lead developer, Darius Jazayeri, joined shortly afterward and merged PIH's own HIV electronic-record work into the new codebase, making OpenMRS a joint project from its earliest months rather than a single institution's tool.

This establishes the founding year, the founding institutions, and the original clinical use case (HIV care in a resource-constrained setting) that shaped the platform's early design. It does not, on its own, establish anything about OpenMRS's current scale or governance — those claims are sourced separately.
