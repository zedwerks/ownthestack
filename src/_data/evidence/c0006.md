Several African national HIV-care programs run on OpenMRS distributions built specifically for that purpose: **KenyaEMR**, developed for the AMPATH partnership and subsequently used more broadly in Kenya's HIV response; a national HIV-care rollout in **Rwanda** dating to 2006; and **UgandaEMR**, a Ministry of Health–mandated OpenMRS distribution that started in HIV care and later expanded into maternal, child-health, and tuberculosis programs. Much of this deployment activity was funded through the **U.S. President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR)**, which financed HIV program-monitoring infrastructure across sub-Saharan Africa and Haiti through the 2000s and 2010s.

This establishes that OpenMRS has operated at national-program scale in specific disease domains (principally HIV, later expanded to maternal/child health and TB) — not that it has operated as a general acute-care hospital system at that scale.
